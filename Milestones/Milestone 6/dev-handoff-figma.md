# Reflection
## What are the biggest challenges in developer handoff, and how can they be avoided?
One of the biggest challenges in developer handoff is miscommunication, when the design looks clear to the designer but isn’t fully understood by developers. This can lead to inconsistencies in spacing, interactions, or component behavior. To avoid this, it’s important to organise the Figma file clearly, add notes for tricky interactions, and do a quick walkthrough together before coding starts.
Before handing off the design, I ran a design kickoff with the developers to ensure we're on the same page and understand the design correctly. I double-check with them about technical feasibility so that we only design what we can build. 
## If a developer misinterprets a design, how should a UX designer clarify?
If a developer misinterprets a design, I’d clarify by showing the intended flow or interaction directly in Figma or sharing a short video. It’s more effective to demonstrate visually than to rely only on written explanations.
## What are the risks of not providing enough detail in a Figma handoff?
Not providing enough detail in a handoff can cause extra rework and delays, since developers might make assumptions or build something that doesn’t match the UX goals. The more context they have, the smoother the process. In my design, I put annotation where it's confusing for developers. I also make a style guide to make it easier for dev to know which typography and colors that I used.
## Why does the developer handoff come after the design kickoff?
The handoff comes after the design kickoff because the kickoff aligns both teams on goals, priorities, and technical limits before design begins. Once everything’s clear and approved, the handoff ensures that what’s been designed can be accurately built.
